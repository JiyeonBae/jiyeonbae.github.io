MyMuse: A Personal Museum for Continued Inquiry and Reflection

Human–AI Interaction · Interactive Visualization · 2026

Under Review at ACM CHI 2027

Overview

MyMuse is an interactive system that supports continued inquiry and reflection after museum visits by connecting artwork photographs with visitors’ personal questions, interpretations, and experiences. It enables visitors to revisit individual artworks and explore recurring interests and relationships across their personal collections.

Motivation

  • Lost Personal Context: Artwork photographs preserve what visitors saw, but not necessarily why they captured a particular work or what it meant to them.
  • Evolving Interpretations: New knowledge and subsequent encounters can prompt visitors to ask new questions and reconsider their earlier interpretations.
  • Hidden Connections Across Artworks: Recurring interests may be reflected in visitors’ questions and interpretations, yet these connections can remain difficult to recognize across separate artwork records.

Research Focus: How can interactive systems support continued inquiry around individual artworks while helping visitors recognize recurring interests and connections across their personal records?

Formative Study

We conducted semi-structured and photo-elicitation interviews with 10 museum visitors, using 100 photographs from their own visits to understand how they recalled, revisited, and reinterpreted artworks.

The study revealed difficulties in recovering the reasons behind photographs, opportunities for reinterpretation through new knowledge, and the importance of considering visitors’ own questions and reflections when examining patterns across collections. These findings informed four design goals.

Design Goals

  • Preserve Personal Contributions: Keep visitors’ own questions and reflections distinguishable from system-generated content.
  • Support Continued Inquiry: Allow visitors to revisit artworks and develop interpretations as their knowledge and experiences change.
  • Ground Reflection in Personal Records: Surface recurring patterns across artworks using evidence from visitors’ own contributions.
  • Connect Individual and Collection-Level Exploration: Enable movement between inquiry about individual artworks and reflection across the collection.

System Design

  • Personal Museum — Artwork-Level Inquiry: Presents photographed artworks in a virtual gallery where visitors can revisit individual works, ask questions, record personal reflections, and explore related artworks.
  • Personal Records & Context-Aware Inquiry: Preserves visitors’ questions and interpretations separately from system-generated content. Using artwork metadata and relevant prior records, MyMuse provides grounded responses that build on earlier inquiries.
  • Interactive Narrative Report — Collection-Level Reflection: Applies narrative visualization principles to organize accumulated personal records through four predefined interpretive lenses—subject, physical characteristics, style and chronology, and creator. Following a guided narrative, visitors can interactively regroup the collection within these lenses and inspect the original remarks supporting the presented relationships.

Technical Approach

  • Grounded Artwork Inquiry: Combines verified artwork metadata, external knowledge sources, and relevant personal records to generate context-aware responses through retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).
  • Personal Meaning Graph: Annotates visitor-authored records using CCO artwork aspects and MyMuEx experiential triggers, enabling retrieval of related questions and reflections across artworks.
  • Evidence-Grounded Narrative Generation: Uses a deterministic planner to select the narrative structure, groupings, and supporting evidence. An LLM phrases the selected observations, while a separate deterministic verifier checks for unsupported claims.

User Study & Results

We conducted two within-subject studies with 16 participants using their own artwork photographs.

  • Vivid Revisitation: 13 of 16 participants favored MyMuse over a conventional per-artwork interface for vividly revisiting their museum experiences in direct comparisons.
  • Reflection Across Artworks: The Narrative Report received significantly higher ratings for recurring-interest awareness, cross-artwork connections, and unexpected discovery than a per-artwork presentation of the same records. The largest effect was observed for cross-artwork connection awareness (M = 6.62 vs. 4.19; Holm-adjusted p = .003, r = .83).

These findings suggest that connecting personal inquiry with reflection across accumulated records can help visitors articulate their interests and recognize relationships among their experiences. The evaluation examined immediate effects rather than long-term changes.

Research Contributions

  • Formative Insights: Identified challenges and opportunities in revisiting artwork photographs and reflecting on personal collections.
  • System Design: Developed a system connecting continued artwork-level inquiry with evidence-grounded, collection-level narrative reflection.
  • Empirical Findings: Provided comparative evidence on how organizing accumulated personal records can support revisitation, recurring-interest awareness, and cross-artwork reflection.
