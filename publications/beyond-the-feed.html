Beyond the Feed: Trajectory-Level Interaction for Understanding and Steering Recommendations Under Short-Term Sensitivity

Human–AI Interaction · Interactive Visualization · Recommender Systems · 2026

Under Review at ACM CHI 2027

Overview

Beyond the Feed introduces trajectory-level interaction, an approach that helps users observe, interpret, and steer evolving recommendation distributions rather than relying solely on item-level feedback. We demonstrate this approach through SteerRec, an interactive system that makes recommendation changes visible and enables users to directly adjust recommendation outputs.

Motivation

  • Short-Term Sensitivity: Brief or incidental interactions can disproportionately shift recommendations toward particular content topics, even when they do not reflect users’ lasting interests.
  • Limited Visibility: Conventional recommendation interfaces show the current feed but provide little insight into how recommendations have changed over time.
  • Indirect User Control: Users often rely on repeated item-level interactions to influence recommendations, without knowing how those actions will affect the overall feed.

Research Focus: How can interactive systems help users understand changes in their recommendation trajectories and deliberately steer recommendations toward their intentions?

Formative Study

We conducted a controlled audit of YouTube and Instagram across 24 runs and a formative survey with 22 participants to investigate short-term sensitivity.

The audit revealed substantial recommendation shifts following brief interactions, while 72.7% of survey participants reported experiencing recommendation drift. These findings highlighted the need for interfaces that make recommendation changes visible and support more explicit user control.

Interaction Framework

We propose an Observe–Interpret–Steer framework that treats evolving recommendation distributions as objects users can inspect and influence.

  • Observe: Make changes in recommendation distributions visible over time.
  • Interpret: Support users in examining potential relationships between their interactions and recommendation shifts.
  • Steer: Enable users to express desired recommendation outcomes and iteratively refine them through feedback.

System Design

  • Trajectory Visualization: Three coordinated views—content space, genre timeline, and interaction timeline—help users examine recommendation distributions, identify emerging patterns, and trace changes over time.
  • LLM-Based Diagnostic Cues: Highlight potential recommendation drift and relate distributional shifts to prior interactions, providing inspectable cues rather than definitive causal explanations.
  • Goal-Based Steering: Allows users to specify desired genre proportions and immediately inspect changes in the displayed recommendation distribution, enabling iterative adjustments.

Technical Approach

  • Calibrated Recommendation Model: Implements a recency-biased recommender calibrated against short-term sensitivity observed in the YouTube and Instagram audit, using a movie dataset with 19 genre categories.
  • Output-Level Steering: Reconfigures displayed recommendations according to user-specified genre weights without modifying the underlying recommender model or requiring access to its internal parameters.

User Study & Results

We conducted a counterbalanced within-subjects study with 20 participants, comparing SteerRec against a conventional feed interface.

  • Recommendation Awareness: Participants reported significantly higher awareness of recommendation changes with SteerRec (M = 5.10 vs. 4.15; Holm-adjusted p = .015, r = .54).
  • Perceived Agency: SteerRec significantly improved participants’ perceived ability to control recommendations (M = 5.30 vs. 3.30; Holm-adjusted p = .001, r = .74).
  • Interaction Trade-offs: Participants valued explicit steering but also identified challenges involving interface complexity and learnability. Many preferred having advanced controls available when needed rather than continuously displayed.

These findings suggest that making recommendation trajectories visible and revisable can support user understanding and agency, while highlighting the need to balance transparency with everyday usability.

My Contributions
