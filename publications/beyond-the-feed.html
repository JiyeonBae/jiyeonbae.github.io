Beyond the Feed: Trajectory-Level Interaction for Understanding and Steering Recommendations Under Short-Term Sensitivity

Human–AI Interaction · Interactive Visualization · Recommender Systems · 2026

Under Review at ACM CHI 2027

Overview

Recommendation systems can rapidly shift toward particular content topics in response to brief or incidental interactions, yet conventional feeds offer little visibility into how these changes unfold over time.

Beyond the Feed introduces trajectory-level interaction, an approach that helps users observe, interpret, and steer evolving recommendation distributions rather than relying solely on item-level feedback. We demonstrate this approach through SteerRec, an interactive research prototype.

Research Focus: How can users understand changes in their recommendation trajectories and deliberately steer recommendations toward their intentions?

Empirical Motivation

We conducted a controlled audit of YouTube and Instagram across 24 runs and a formative survey with 22 participants to investigate short-term sensitivity in recommendation systems.

The audit revealed substantial recommendation shifts following brief interactions, while 72.7% of survey participants reported experiencing recommendation drift. These findings highlighted a gap between users’ awareness of recommendation changes and their ability to understand or control them.

Interaction Framework

We propose an Observe–Interpret–Steer framework that treats evolving recommendation distributions as objects users can inspect and influence.

  • Observe: Make changes in recommendation distributions visible over time.
  • Interpret: Support users in understanding potential relationships between their interactions and recommendation shifts.
  • Steer: Enable users to express desired recommendation outcomes and iteratively refine them through feedback.

Key Features

  • Trajectory Visualization: Three coordinated views display content distributions, genre trends, and interaction history, helping users track recommendation changes over time.
  • LLM-Based Diagnostic Cues: Highlight potential recommendation drift and relate distributional shifts to prior interactions, supporting users’ own interpretations.
  • Goal-Based Steering: Allows users to specify desired genre proportions and immediately inspect the resulting recommendation distribution, without modifying the underlying recommender model.

Evaluation & Findings

We conducted a counterbalanced within-subjects study with 20 participants, comparing SteerRec against a conventional feed-only interface.

  • Recommendation Awareness: Participants reported significantly greater awareness of recommendation changes with SteerRec (M = 5.10 vs. 4.15; Holm-adjusted p = .015).
  • Perceived Agency: SteerRec significantly improved participants’ perceived ability to control recommendations (M = 5.30 vs. 3.30; Holm-adjusted p = .001).
  • Interaction Trade-offs: Participants valued explicit steering but also identified challenges involving interface complexity and learnability.

These findings suggest that making recommendation trajectories visible and revisable can support user understanding and agency, while highlighting the importance of balancing transparency with everyday usability.

My Contributions

I contributed to the concept of enabling users to directly steer recommendation outputs, designed SteerRec’s interactive visualizations, and conducted user-study sessions.
